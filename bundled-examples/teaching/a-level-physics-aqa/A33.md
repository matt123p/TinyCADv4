## From a smooth signal to a code

**Post-16 · ages 16–19**

The ADC measures VIN at clock edges and produces a four-bit number. The DAC turns that number back into a voltage with a finite number of steps.

### Your investigation

Plot VIN and OUT together. Count the levels, estimate the quantisation error and change the clock rate. Compare the bit signals with the serial encoder output.

### This activity

Draw an annotated sampler/ADC/encoding chain and identify where timing and voltage resolution enter. A working converter demonstration needs compatible mixed-signal models or external processing. Scope display sampling must not be described as an experiment on a modelled ADC.

### Getting started

Open the Simulation tab and press Run. Select a source or component to see its values. Read the labelled signals, then change one quantity at a time. Equal net labels identify connected points.

ADC data bits BIT0–BIT3 feed both the DAC and the educational PCM encoder. Compare VIN with the reconstructed staircase OUT.

**Explain:** Use an equation, a truth table or a state sequence to justify your prediction. Include units and discuss the model assumptions.

The model predicts electrical behaviour; it does not replace real practical measurements.
