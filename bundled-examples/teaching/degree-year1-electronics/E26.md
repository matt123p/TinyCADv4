## A sensor switches an output

**First-year degree · ages 18+**

The thermistor and fixed resistor make a temperature-dependent voltage. The comparator compares it with a reference; the transistor supplies the LED current.

### Your investigation

Predict which temperature direction turns the LED on. Change Temperature, rerun, then change the reference. Explain why a real alarm may need hysteresis.

### This activity

Combine a defined input, conditioner and output stage. Save analytical targets, model identities, captures, loading assumptions and a plan for bench comparison. The project brief is our proposal, not an institution-prescribed assignment.

### Getting started

Open the Simulation tab and press Run. Select a source or component to see its values. Read the labelled signals, then change one quantity at a time. Equal net labels identify connected points.

Raise the NTC Temperature above 25 °C and rerun. The falling sensor voltage switches the comparator, then the transistor drives the LED. Adjust THRESHOLD to choose the switching temperature.

**Explain:** Use an equation, a truth table or a state sequence to justify your prediction. Include units and discuss the model assumptions.

The model predicts electrical behaviour; it does not replace real practical measurements.
